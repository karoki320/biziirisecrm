Instagram is the best shop window in Kenya and one of the worst shops. It is superb at getting a stranger to notice you and hopeless at everything that happens after they decide to buy.

Most businesses we build stores for did not need one when they started. They needed one about eight months later, and nobody told them the moment had arrived. Here is how to tell.

## Five signs you have outgrown the DMs

**1. You are answering the same three questions forty times a week.** How much. Do you deliver to Kisumu. Is it available in black. Every one of those is a question a product page answers once, permanently, while you sleep.

**2. Orders arrive at 11pm and go cold by morning.** Someone decided to buy. They messaged. You were asleep. By 9am they have scrolled past three competitors. This is the expensive one, and you will never see it in your numbers because a lost sale leaves no trace.

**3. You cannot say what your bestseller is.** Not with confidence, not with a number. You have a feeling. The feeling is usually wrong, and it is quietly driving what you restock.

**4. Payment is a screenshot.** Customer sends money, screenshots the confirmation, you scroll back through a chat to match it to an order. At five orders a day this is mildly annoying. At fifty it is an evening's work and a source of genuine disputes.

**5. Someone else has to help, and they cannot.** The moment you want to hand ordering to an assistant, you discover the entire system lives in your head and your phone. You cannot delegate a DM inbox.

If three of those are true, the DMs are already costing you more than a store would.

## What actually changes

Not the marketing. You will still post, still reel, still reply. What changes is what happens in the ninety seconds after someone decides.

**Before:** they message, wait for you, ask the price, wait, ask about delivery, wait, send M-Pesa to your personal number, screenshot it, wait for confirmation. Six touches, and you are personally required for five of them.

**After:** they tap the link in your bio, see the price and delivery cost, pay with an STK prompt on their handset, and get a receipt. You get a notification. You were not involved.

The store does not sell for you. It stops you from being the bottleneck between wanting to buy and buying.

## The thing nobody expects: the data

Ask any of our shop clients what surprised them and it is never the sales. It is finding out what actually sells.

A store tells you which product was viewed two hundred times and bought twice — which is a pricing or photo problem, not a demand problem. It tells you which item quietly produces a third of your revenue while you have been posting about something else. It tells you which delivery areas order repeatedly, so you know where to push.

Instagram tells you what got likes. Likes and purchases are different populations, and running a business on the first while being paid by the second is how you end up with a stockroom full of the wrong thing.

## What it costs, honestly

Kenyan agencies commonly publish **KES 100,000 to 400,000** for an ecommerce build. We start at **KES 35,000** for a shop with M-Pesa checkout, a product catalogue and an admin dashboard you can actually run yourself.

What moves the price is not how pretty it is. It is:

- **How many products**, and whether they have variants — sizes, colours, weights
- **Delivery**, and whether one flat rate will do or you need zones
- **Stock**, and whether you need the system to track it and warn you
- **Customer accounts**, if repeat buyers should see their order history

Start smaller than you think. A shop with your thirty best products that goes live next week beats a complete catalogue that goes live in April.

## Keep the WhatsApp button

One thing we insist on, because it works: keep a WhatsApp button on every product page.

Plenty of Kenyan buyers, especially on a first purchase from a business they do not know, want to ask a human before they pay. Forcing them through checkout loses that sale. Letting them message keeps it, and they usually buy the second time without asking.

The store is not there to stop people talking to you. It is there so the ones who do not need to talk to you do not have to wait for you.

## When you should not build one

Be honest about this, because we would rather tell you now.

If you are getting under five orders a week, the DMs are fine. Put the effort into content and getting found. A store with no traffic is a beautifully built empty room.

If your products are genuinely bespoke — every job quoted individually — you do not need a checkout. You need a good enquiry page and fast replies.

If you cannot commit to keeping stock and prices current, an out-of-date store is worse than no store. Nothing kills trust faster than paying for something that turns out to be unavailable.

Everyone else: the signs at the top of this page are a checklist. Three or more and you are paying for the store already. You are just paying in lost midnight orders instead of shillings.
